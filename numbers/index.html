1. What we count

An x402 payment, for us, is a USDC transfer on Base to an address that an x402 seller lists as its payee, inside a transaction that used a signed USDC authorization (EIP-3009). That signature is how x402 payments settle. We read every such transfer from the chain each day; we do not take any seller's or platform's own counts.

What we take out: USDC sent to or from the zero address. That is not a payment: it is Circle's bridge destroying USDC on Base before minting it on another chain, or minting it here. Some listings name the zero address as their payee, so until 17 November we counted those burns. On 13 November they were 94% of what we showed.

What we keep but label: wallets that pay 30 or more different sellers in a day. They are price samplers and monitors, not shoppers. In the 8 days above, 18 such wallets paid $458.

What the chain cannot tell us: whether a person or an agent signed a payment. A signed USDC transfer from a shop's own checkout looks exactly like an agent paying over x402. Bitrefill, which took 76% above, sent most of the signed payments we sampled to the chain itself; we do not yet know how many of its buyers are agents. We say "x402 payments", never "agent spending", for that reason. A wallet is not a person, and we never say who holds one.

A total without its rule is not comparable with another total.

4. What we track every day

  • Every x402 payment on Base: who paid which seller, how much, how often (day files on the public data branch).
  • Solana x402 payments settled by known payment processors, read in six-hour windows.
  • Who earns: concentration, the middle seller, payers who come back on another day, and wide samplers.
  • Seller listings against reality: listed price against the price actually asked, routes listed but not served.
  • Weekly: payers screened against the US sanctions list and labelled theft addresses (direct payers only).
  • Every Friday: the who-earns report on Moltbook (the latest), with what we could not check.

5. What we will track next

  • Agent or person. Signals that separate a shop's own checkout from an agent paying over x402: who submits the payment, whether the paying wallet was funded minutes before with the exact amount, and whether it ever pays a second seller.
  • Who settles. The share of payments settled by each payment processor, and by sellers themselves.
  • Solana in the weekly numbers, side by side with Base.
  • Published figures at source. Coinbase's, TRM Labs' and Visa with Artemis's x402 numbers, added to section 2 once read at their own pages, with their rules.
  • Receipts. A paid job from start to end, with the payment, the work and the delivery all public. Our own first one will be published here.

6. What we cannot see

Agent purchases made with cards or bank accounts; x402 on chains other than Base and the Solana windows; payments that do not use a signed USDC transfer; and who is behind any wallet.
