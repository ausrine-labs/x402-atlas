A shop, a crowd, and a seller that left

2. The crowds pay once

On 19 November ax1's paying wallets jumped from a few hundred a day to 2,307, then 3,376 on the 21st, while its money fell: the middle wallet paid 2¢, one call. 1,802 of the wallets on the 19th had never paid ax1 before. ax1 runs paid quests with prize pools, so this looks like quest traffic, not use.

A smaller seller, keyring-agent.blockchhub.link, shows the same shape every day: about 850 wallets, each paying exactly once, 5¢. Over 8 days 6,806 wallets, 2 of which came back.

For anyone ranking sellers by "unique payers": this week the two biggest crowds in x402 are wallets that pay one call each. Count the wallets that come back on another day; for ax1 that is 2,887, for keyring-agent it is 2.

3. The steadiest seller left x402

dtelecom sold speech-to-text, voice and voice rooms per minute. From 10 to 18 November its 25 paying wallets all came back day after day, about $1,100 a day. On 19 November it dropped out of the x402 registry, and its payee has received no x402 payment since. Its own service page now prices everything in prepaid credits. We do not know why. The one seller of the autumn with a loyal, paying set of agents chose to move them off pay-per-call.

What we checked: dtelecom was in the registry snapshot of each day to 18 Nov and in none from 19 Nov; the payee address it listed received no x402 payment from 19 to 22 Nov; dtelecom's public service list (x402.dtelecom.org/.well-known/x402), read 23 Nov, gives prices in microcredits. We have not asked dtelecom.

5. What it means

  • Sellers: what sells is what people already buy (gift cards) or what an agent needs every day (dtelecom's voice minutes, model calls). One-off utilities earn cents.
  • Investors and reporters: any x402 total for November is mostly Bitrefill. Ask for the figure without it, and for returning payers, not unique ones.
  • Watch: whether other sellers with regular agent customers follow dtelecom to prepaid credits. If they do, pay-per-call is the way in, not the way agents keep paying.
